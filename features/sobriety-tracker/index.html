CannaClear feature

Keep your cannabis-free progress visible

CannaClear turns your quit date into a clear, personal record of cannabis-free days and milestones. It is designed to help you notice progress without treating a streak as a measure of your worth.

Transparent by design: educational recovery support, not diagnosis or treatment. Claims on this page distinguish product functions from general research evidence.

What this feature does

A focused set of tools for recording and understanding your own recovery experience.

Count cannabis-free days

Set your quit date and see the time that has passed in one consistent place.

Mark meaningful milestones

Use visible milestones as reminders of the work you have already done.

Continue after a difficult day

A lapse can be recorded honestly without turning the entire recovery process into a pass-or-fail score.

How to use it responsibly

The most useful record is consistent enough to reveal context, but flexible enough to reflect real life.

A record, not a judgment

A sobriety counter can make an abstract goal more concrete. CannaClear shows elapsed time and milestones so you can review progress at a glance. The tracker does not decide whether your recovery is successful, and it does not replace a treatment plan.

  • Choose a quit date that reflects your actual plan.
  • Review progress when motivation feels less immediate.
  • Use milestones as context alongside sleep, mood, cravings, and support.

Research context and limitations

CannaClear uses established behavior-change concepts as product design inputs. That is different from claiming that this app has proven clinical efficacy.

Evidence-aware product design

Self-monitoring and feedback are common behavior-change techniques in digital health tools. A 2024 systematic review of digital cannabis interventions found that feedback on behavior was frequently used, while also concluding that more high-quality research is needed. This supports the design principle behind visible progress; it does not establish that CannaClear itself causes abstinence.

Important distinction: This research does not establish clinical efficacy for CannaClear, and CannaClear has not been presented as a clinically validated treatment.

Research used for context: 2024 systematic review of digital cannabis interventions; 2023 systematic review of behavior-change techniques and app engagement; systematic review of self-monitoring in substance use.

What this feature does not do

The counter reflects the date and information you enter. It cannot verify abstinence, predict relapse, or assess cannabis use disorder. A reset is data, not a diagnosis or a moral verdict.
