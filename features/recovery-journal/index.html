How to use it responsibly

The most useful record is consistent enough to reveal context, but flexible enough to reflect real life.

Reflection with structure

A journal can help separate what happened from what you felt and what you did next. Structured prompts make entries easier to compare, while free-form notes preserve the context that matters to you.

  • Keep entries brief enough to sustain.
  • Record triggers without blaming yourself for having them.
  • Review patterns periodically rather than treating each day as a verdict.

Research context and limitations

CannaClear uses established behavior-change concepts as product design inputs. That is different from claiming that this app has proven clinical efficacy.

Evidence-aware product design

Self-monitoring, feedback, prompts, and goal setting are frequently used behavior-change techniques in mobile health apps. Research can explain why these components are plausible design choices, but it does not show that journaling alone prevents relapse or that CannaClear is equivalent to therapy.

Important distinction: This research does not establish clinical efficacy for CannaClear, and CannaClear has not been presented as a clinically validated treatment.

Research used for context: 2024 systematic review of digital cannabis interventions; 2023 systematic review of behavior-change techniques and app engagement; systematic review of self-monitoring in substance use.

What this feature does not do

Journal prompts are educational and reflective. They do not interpret your mental health, diagnose a condition, or provide confidential therapy. App progress data is described as primarily local in our Privacy Policy; review that policy for the current details and exceptions.
