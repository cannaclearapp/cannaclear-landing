A calmer next step when a craving hits

The CannaClear SOS area offers guided breathing, grounding ideas, and simple prompts for the immediate moment. It is designed to reduce decision load while you choose what to do next.

What this feature does

A focused set of tools for recording and understanding your own recovery experience.

Open one clear action

Move from an urge to a concrete coping step without searching through a long article.

Follow a breathing prompt

Use guided box breathing as a structured pause, if breathing exercises feel appropriate for you.

Reconnect with your plan

Create enough space to choose support, change your environment, or revisit your reasons for quitting.

How to use it responsibly

The most useful record is consistent enough to reveal context, but flexible enough to reflect real life.

Support for the moment, not emergency care

Cravings can narrow attention and make familiar responses feel automatic. The SOS tools organize simple coping options in one place. They do not guarantee that an urge will stop, and different techniques work differently for different people.

  • Open the tool early rather than waiting for the urge to feel unmanageable.
  • Pair an in-app prompt with a real-world action such as leaving a trigger situation.
  • Contact a trusted person or professional when self-guided tools are not enough.

Research context and limitations

CannaClear uses established behavior-change concepts as product design inputs. That is different from claiming that this app has proven clinical efficacy.

Evidence-aware product design

Behavioral approaches to cannabis use commonly include identifying triggers and practicing coping responses. Reviews of digital interventions suggest potential benefits, but interventions vary and the evidence does not validate every individual feature. CannaClear's SOS tools are practical prompts, not a clinically tested treatment protocol.

Important distinction: This research does not establish clinical efficacy for CannaClear, and CannaClear has not been presented as a clinically validated treatment.

Research used for context: 2024 systematic review of digital cannabis interventions; 2023 systematic review of behavior-change techniques and app engagement; systematic review of self-monitoring in substance use.

What this feature does not do

The SOS screen is not a crisis service, medical treatment, or substitute for professional help. If you may harm yourself or someone else, or face a medical emergency, contact local emergency services immediately.
