About CannaClear

CannaClear exists to make cannabis recovery feel less lonely, less confusing, and more practical. We want the experience of quitting weed to feel supported by real structure, honest guidance, and tools that help people keep going when recovery feels slow.

Our Mission

Our mission is to help people quit cannabis and stay motivated throughout withdrawal, early recovery, and long-term change. We focus on making recovery easier to understand and easier to stick with.

That means giving readers realistic explanations of what may happen, practical steps they can use today, and enough context to recognize when professional support is the right next step. We avoid fear-based language, miracle claims, and rigid promises about how quickly someone should recover.

Why CannaClear Exists

Many people trying to quit weed find a gap between what recovery feels like and what most internet content explains. They may be dealing with cravings, sleep disruption, low motivation, anxiety, emotional flatness, or setbacks, but the guidance available often feels shallow, generic, or overly alarmist.

CannaClear was built to close that gap with educational content and practical tracking tools that treat recovery as a real process instead of a simple motivational moment.

The website and app are designed to complement each other. The guides explain common recovery patterns and the app helps users record their own progress. Neither is intended to diagnose a condition or replace care from a qualified professional.

Meet Lukas Pietruschka

Lukas Pietruschka is the founder of CannaClear. He works at the intersection of recovery research, product design, and behavior change.

His focus is helping people understand cannabis withdrawal, dopamine recovery, habit formation, behavioral psychology, and the longer emotional arc of quitting weed. His goal is to translate complex research into language that feels clear, grounded, and actually useful in everyday life.

Lukas is not a physician, psychologist, or licensed addiction clinician. His role is that of a founder, researcher, and product builder. CannaClear identifies that boundary clearly because lived and product experience can improve educational support, but it does not replace clinical expertise.

Articles are published under his name when he is responsible for researching, writing, or substantively editing them. A medical-review label will only be displayed if a suitably qualified professional has actually reviewed the specific article.

How We Research

We begin with the question a person in recovery is trying to answer. We then review relevant scientific literature, systematic reviews, clinical guidance, and public health sources. We prioritize original research and authoritative institutions over summaries from commercial websites.

Sources commonly include PubMed-indexed journals, the National Institute on Drug Abuse, the National Institutes of Health, SAMHSA, the CDC, the World Health Organization, and other established scientific or public health bodies. References are listed on articles so readers can inspect the evidence themselves.

Research about cannabis recovery has limits. Study populations, patterns of use, THC potency, co-occurring conditions, and outcome measures vary. When evidence is uncertain or individual timelines differ widely, we aim to say so instead of presenting an estimate as a guarantee.

Community experiences may help us identify questions, confusing language, or practical challenges worth addressing. They are not treated as proof of a medical claim and do not replace peer-reviewed evidence.

Read the full Editorial Policy for our source selection, fact-checking, update, and correction process.

Our Values

Clarity. Recovery information should be understandable without stripping away important nuance.

Transparency. We identify who creates the content, what sources support it, and where our expertise ends.

Respect. People can have different goals, histories, and reasons for changing their cannabis use. Our language should inform without shaming.

Evidence with context. We use research to guide our content while acknowledging uncertainty and individual variation.

Privacy. Recovery is personal. We aim to collect only the data needed to provide the product and explain our practices clearly in our Privacy Policy.

Helping People Recover

Recovery is not only about stopping a behavior. It is also about rebuilding routines, understanding triggers, making progress visible, and learning how to stay steady through difficult phases. CannaClear is built to support that wider process.

Our educational guides cover withdrawal, cravings, sleep, mood, cognitive recovery, motivation, relapse prevention, and cannabis detection. The app helps users track sober days, symptoms, cravings, money saved, and personal milestones.

We do not promise that an app or article will produce a particular outcome. CannaClear is one support tool, and many people benefit from combining self-guided resources with trusted friends, peer support, counseling, or medical care.

Corrections and Accountability

Accuracy matters, especially when content touches health and recovery. If we confirm a substantive error, we correct the article and review nearby claims that may rely on the same information. Important updates may be reflected in the article's updated date.

Readers can report a concern by emailing us with the page URL, the passage in question, and any supporting source. We review factual feedback separately from general product support.
